Start with the problem. Then put AI to work on it.

For mid-market companies without an AI team. Tell me what's slow, expensive or stuck. I'll say plainly whether AI can help, then build it, connect it to the systems you run, and keep your people in charge. One project at a time, or as a part-time head of AI.

Problems people bring me

  • "Nobody can see the whole picture."Data agents pull every system into one set of agreed numbers and one dashboard.
  • "My people retype the same information all day."An agent prepares the order, quote, claim or reconciliation. A person reviews and sends.
  • "The board wants an AI plan."A written, ranked list of what's worth doing, and what to leave alone.
  • "We bought AI tools and nothing changed."Three real jobs, each with an owner, rules and a measure.
  • "I need someone to own AI."A part-time head of AI, a set number of days a month.

How it stays under control

  • A written charterOne plain-language page: what the agent may read, change, and never do. You approve it first.
  • Earned autonomyEvery agent starts by observing and moves up on its record. Most stay at Draft.
  • Human gatesAnything that reaches a customer, moves money or can't be undone waits for a person.
  • A log nobody can editEvery action, refusal and approval, written once.

How it starts

A 30-minute call about one job. If the honest answer is "don't," you hear it on that call. The Readiness Review comes next, and it is a natural place to stop if you would rather build in-house.
