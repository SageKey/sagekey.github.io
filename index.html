    How it stays safe

    Getting an agent to do the work is the easy part.

    Most AI pilots don't fail on the task. They stall on four questions nobody can answer. Every agent I build answers all four before it starts.

    "What can it touch?"

    A written charter.

    One plain-language page that says what the agent may read, may change, and may never do. Your team approves it before anything is built, and it belongs to you.

    "How far do we trust it?"

    It starts at the bottom.

    Every agent begins by observing and moves up one level at a time, on its record. Most never need to leave Draft. The top level is empty on purpose.

    Prepares the work: the order, the quote, the reply. A person reviews it and sends it. Most of the value is here.

    "What if it's wrong?"

    A person at every exit.

    Anything that reaches a customer, moves money or can't be undone stops and waits. Each item shows the finished work and the rule that stopped it, so approving is a real decision and not a rubber stamp.

    Fair questions

    What you're probably thinking.

    We tried AI and it produced something embarrassing.

    Then the pilot was missing a gate, not a better model. My approach assumes the model will be confidently wrong on a regular basis. That's the design premise, not an edge case. The agent drafts, a person sends, and the log records which person. An agent at Draft can't embarrass you in front of a customer, because it never reaches one unaccompanied.

    Our data is a mess, and half of it lives in the ERP.

    That's the normal starting condition, and it's most of my background: years of moving order flow, shipping and inventory between systems that were never designed to talk to each other. If the data turns out to be the real problem, the Readiness Review will say so, and that's worth knowing before you spend on anything else.

    Is this about replacing our people?

    Not the way I scope it. The work that suits an agent is the work nobody wants: retyping, copying between two screens, the reconciliation that eats a morning. The Draft level keeps a person in the loop on purpose, and makes them the reviewer instead of the typist.

    You're one person. What if you disappear?

    It's the reason everything is built to be handed over. The agent's rules are plain-language documents you own. They're enforced by ordinary software your team can read, not by prompts only I understand. The oversight screen is for your team, not for me. If I vanish, you have a running system and the documents that explain it.
