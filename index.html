    How it stays safe

    How an agent is kept under control.

    Before an agent starts, four questions get written answers.

    "What can it touch?"

    A written charter.

    One plain-language page that says what the agent may read, may change, and may never do. Your team approves it before anything is built, and it belongs to you.

    "How far do we trust it?"

    It starts at the bottom.

    Every agent begins by observing and moves up one level at a time, on its record. The top level is left empty.

    Prepares the work: the order, the quote, the reply. A person reviews it and sends it. Most of the value is here.

    Anything that reaches a customer, moves money or can't be undone stops and waits. Each item shows the finished work and the rule that stopped it, so the reviewer can see what they are approving.

    Fair questions

    Common questions.

    We're not a manufacturer. Is this for us?

    Most likely. People retyping between systems, numbers nobody trusts and AI tools nobody owns look much the same in any mid-market company. My deepest systems experience is in manufacturing and distribution.

    We tried AI and it produced something embarrassing.

    The model will sometimes be confidently wrong, so the agent only drafts. A person sends, and the log records who. Nothing an agent drafts reaches a customer without that person.

    Our data is a mess, and half of it lives in the ERP.

    That's the normal starting condition, and it's most of my background: years of moving order flow, shipping and inventory between systems that were never designed to talk to each other. If the data turns out to be the real problem, the Readiness Review will say so, and that's worth knowing before you spend on anything else.

    Is this about replacing our people?

    Not the way I scope it. The work that suits an agent is the work nobody wants: retyping, copying between two screens, the reconciliation that eats a morning. A person stays in the loop as the reviewer instead of the typist.

    You're one person. What if you disappear?

    It's the reason everything is built to be handed over. The agent's rules are plain-language documents you own. They're enforced by ordinary code your team can read, and your team runs the oversight screen. If I vanish, you have a running system and the documents that explain it.
